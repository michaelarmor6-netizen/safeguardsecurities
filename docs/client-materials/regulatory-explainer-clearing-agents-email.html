Natural-Person Third-Party Clearing Agents

A regulatory explainer for stakeholders, compliance officers, and regulatory reviewers.

Dear {{firstName}},

A third-party clearing agent handles trade settlement, reconciliation, and custody instructions on another party's behalf. That role can sit with a firm — or with an individual acting in a licensed professional capacity, typically under a clearing agreement or a registered firm.

The point of this briefing is a single principle:

Clearing is a licensed activity, not a corporate privilege. A natural person performing it is a regulated financial intermediary, with the same obligations as a clearing firm — and with liability that is personal.

How individuals are classified

Whether independent or operating under a clearing agreement, an individual performing clearing functions is regulated under one or more established roles: registered representative, introducing broker, associated person, or fiduciary custodian — registered with the relevant authority (SEC, FINRA, CFTC, BSP, or local equivalent) before performing any clearing function, holding credentials such as Series 7, Series 3, or AP registration.

The six core obligations

1 · Licensing & registration. Appropriate credentials and registration with the relevant authority come first — no registration, no clearing.

2 · KYC / AML. Client verification, money-laundering monitoring, suspicious-activity reporting, and ongoing due diligence — with compliance logs maintained even without corporate infrastructure.

3 · Segregation of client funds. Client money sits in segregated accounts, never personal ones. In most jurisdictions, failure to segregate is a criminal offense.

4 · Custody & recordkeeping. Execution records, settlement confirmations, custody instructions, communications, and audit trails — retained for multi-year periods set by rule (e.g. up to six years under SEC 17a-4; five under CFTC 1.31; local rules vary).

5 · Fiduciary duty. Act in the client's best interest, avoid and disclose conflicts, disclose risk. Violations carry civil and criminal penalties — borne personally.

6 · Reporting. Daily trade activity, monthly reconciliations, annual compliance audits, and conflict-of-interest disclosures keep the role transparent to regulators.

Why regulators permit it

Because oversight attaches to the activity, frameworks are built to support both corporate and individual intermediaries — as markets historically did with floor traders and associated persons. If anything, the individual route strengthens accountability: obligations are identical to a firm's, but liability is personal.

At a glance

Requirement Applies to individuals? Why
Licensing Yes Clearing is a regulated activity
KYC / AML Yes Prevent fraud & laundering
Segregated funds Yes Protect client assets
Recordkeeping Yes Audit & compliance
Fiduciary duty Yes Client protection
Reporting Yes Market integrity

The bottom line: a natural person acting as a third-party clearing agent is held to the full standard of a clearing firm — licensing, KYC/AML, segregated funds, custody records, fiduciary duty, and regulatory reporting. The structure changes; the obligations do not.
